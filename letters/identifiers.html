    Chapter 2 Identifiers and referencing mechanisms

    Identifiers, represented as @xml:id attributes on appropriate elements follow a consistent naming scheme across the edition: letters, invoices, articles and annotation layers.

    • Collection siglum: e.g. SL for the entire Szymon Laks' correspondence corpus.
    • Text number: sequential number, padded to assure correct sorting; in the Szymon Laks correspondence these are:
      • Letter number: sequential number, padded to 3 digits, e.g. 001 for Letter no. 1
      • Invoice number: sequential number, padded to 2 digits and prefixed with A, e.g. A01 for Invoice no. 1
    • Text identifier: expressed as @xml:id attribute on TEI, constructed as corpus siglum followed by the tex number
      • Letter identifier: corpus siglum followed by the letter number, e.g. SL-001 for Letter no. 1
      • Invoice identifier: corpus siglum followed by the invoice number, e.g. SL-A01
    • Note identifiers are built on a pattern text-identifier-note-note-number (for notes in the main text of the letter) or text-identifier-attachment-note-note-number (for notes in letter attachments): e.g. SL-008-note-2, SL-008-attachment-note-1. Referenced as the value of @corresp on anchor.

    Identifiers are defined exactly once across the dataset, where they occur as a value of @xml:id attribute on an XML element

    Identifiers may be used multiple times when they are referenced from another element. This may be done via a number of pointing attributes, such as @corresp, @target, etc. Each of these attributes has a specific function across the dataset.

    2.1 Registers

    References for analytical features can be resolved against appropriate registers.

    • bibl.xml: bibliography
    • organizations.xml: register of organizations mentioned in the correspondenc
    • persons.xml: register of people
    • places.xml: register of places and locations
    • taxonomy.xml: metadata classifications
