    Chapter 5 Critical apparatus

    All editorial work leading to establishing the transcribed text should be explicitly marked up with adequate TEI mechanisms.

    • abbr, expan, ex: abbreviations and their expansions
    • sic, corr: scribal/printing errors and their corrections
    • orig, reg: regularization of spelling and punctuation
    • app: record of alternate readings across extant witnesses
    • supplied: editorial conjectures
    • note: marginalia (outside of the linear, logical flow of the text)

    Given that all the sources were printed and multiple copies are available, it's not likely that the following phaenomena occur:

    • gap: lacunae
    • unclear: uncertain passages
    • del: deletions
    • add: additions
    • subst: substitutions (logically connected additions and deletions)

    Where appropriate, these mechanisms should be combined to represent editorial acts explicitly and unambiguously, for example unclear in an apparatus rdg. In particular, the choice element should be used specifically to explicitly pair the alternate source- and edited- variants (logically connected abbreviations and their expansions, errors and corrections, original and regularized forms).

    5.1 Abbreviations

    Abbreviations in the primary source should be dealt at the word- or phrase- level, as logically appropriate for the passage in question. These should be wrapped in a choice with abbr (for the original, abbreviated) and expan (for the expanded) form.

    <choice>
  <abbr>R.P.</abbr>
  <expan>Rzeczpospolitą</expan>
</choice>

    5.2 Correction of errors

    Scribal errors and printing mistakes should be handled with the sic (for the source version) and corr (for the corrected form), wrapped in a choice element.

    <choice>
  <sic>czarrów</sic>
  <corr>czartów</corr>
</choice>

    5.3 Spelling regularization (choice)

    Words or phrases where spelling of the primary source differs from the transcription are wrapped in a choice with orig (for the transliteration) and reg (for transcription). In principle this should be kept at the word- level, which in practice often means word-level in the primary source, but a longer phrase after regularization.

    <choice>
  <orig>servvs</orig>
  <reg>servus</reg>
</choice>

    5.4 Punctuation

    Punctuation should only be marked up explicitly where it requires regularization, and wrapped in choice in such cases.

    5.5 Variant readings (app)

    Phrases with variant readings attested by different witnesses should be explicitly encoded with the app element, with as many nested rdg elements, as there are variants.

    Each of the rdg MUST have a @wit attribute providing sigla (identifiers) of ALL witnesses attesting this reading.

    A single reading, selected by the editor, MUST have a @type attribute set to lemma to indicate the edition's preferred reading. The use of lemma is not allowed in this customization!

    <app n="2">
  <rdg type="lem" wit="#ZS1610 #ZS1592 #ZS1598 #ZS1601 #ZS1603">więtszych</rdg>
  <rdg wit="#ZS1579 #ZS1585">więtszej</rdg>
</app>

    Where certain witnesses omit the passage in question, an empty rdg should be given.

    <app n="1">
  <rdg type="lem" wit="#ZS1610 #ZS1592 #ZS1598 #ZS1601 #ZS1603"/>
  <rdg wit="#ZS1579 #ZS1585">świętego </rdg>
</app>

    5.6 Conjectures (supplied)

    Editorial conjectures (passages not present in the source text) must be explicitly marked with supplied.

    A particular type of conjecture is needed for the purpose of providing consistent navigation structures for the text. Since every div MUST have a heading, there's a need to supply it every time when the source doesn't.

    <head>
  <supplied>Żywot świętego Fryderyka</supplied>
</head>

    5.7 Marginalia (note)

    Marginalia, representing secondary headings (arguments) or references to Bible and patristic works, are encoded with note with @place set to margin and @type set to one of argument, ref or other.

    In rare cases where the marginale is supplied by the editor, it will be explicitly marked with @resp set to editor.

    <note type="ref" resp="editor" place="margin" subtype="bible" corresp="BKWPCTUK">Ps 42(41),2</note>

    note may also be used to encode editorial commentary, see the corresponding chapter
