    Chapter 7 Editorial commentary (linked)

    Editorial commentary is always linked to the place it refers to, using note or cit elements as wrappers, and @corresp as the reference.

    Commentary is often linked to note elements representing marginalia, as below:

    <note type="ref" subtype="bible" place="margin" corresp="BCAVCMJY">Lev. 18.</note>
<note type="ref" subtype="patristic" place="margin" corresp="QFUNMKKW">Moral. 14. cap. 34.</note>

    It may also be added in arbitrary places, as below:

    <note corresp="XPAHNHAU"/>

    Commentary may also be attached to explicit quotes in the source text.

    <cit type="bible" corresp="BHXVPAKN">
  <quote>Powstanie naród na naród, królestwo na królestwo, powstaną synowie na
ojce</quote>
</cit>

    In rare cases where the marginale is supplied by the editor, it will be explicitly marked with @resp set to editor.

    <note type="ref" resp="editor" place="margin" subtype="bible" corresp="BKWPCTUK">Ps 42(41),2</note>

    7.1 Resolving linked references

    References can be resolved against specific registers, depending on the presence and value of other attributes, particularly @type and @subtype.

    • biblicalReferences biblical references (with bibl as note/@subtype or cit/@type
    • bibl non-biblical (patristic and other) references (with patristic or other as note/@subtype or cit/@type
    • comment-context where no @type is present
